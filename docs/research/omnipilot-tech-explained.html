The Big Picture: What Is OmniPilot?

Think of it like this

Imagine you had a personal secretary who:

  • Sits in every meeting with you and takes notes
  • Remembers every conversation you've ever had
  • Can answer "What did I discuss with X about Y last week?"
  • Gives you a summary of your day every evening
  • Never shares your information with anyone

That's OmniPilot. Except the "secretary" is software running entirely on your Mac.

What You Just Built

A Mac menu bar app that:

  1. Listens to your microphone (always-on or push-to-talk)
  2. Detects when someone is speaking (ignores silence)

The Pipeline: How It All Connects

What is a Pipeline?

A pipeline is a series of steps where the output of one step becomes the input of the next. Like a factory assembly line — raw material (your voice) goes in one end, and a finished product (stored, searchable memory) comes out the other.

Technology 1: Swift (The App)

Real-world analogy

Swift is the language we used to talk to your Mac. Just like Hindi or English is how you talk to people, Swift is how programmers talk to Apple devices. It's the official language Apple created for building Mac, iPhone, and iPad apps.

Why Swift?
  • It's Apple's own language — best access to Mac features (microphone, menu bar, notifications)
  • It's fast — runs at near-C speed, important for real-time audio processing
  • SwiftUI — Apple's UI framework makes building the popover/settings very easy
  • No alternative — to put an icon in your Mac's menu bar, you need Swift or Objective-C

What Is SwiftUI?

SwiftUI is Apple's modern way of building user interfaces. Instead of dragging buttons around in a visual editor, you describe what you want in code:

// "I want a text field and a button side by side" HStack { TextField("Ask me anything...", text: $query) Button("Ask") { askQuestion() } }

SwiftUI then figures out how to render it on screen. It's like describing a room to an interior designer instead of placing every piece of furniture yourself.

What Is a Menu Bar App?

Menu bar apps are the small icons in the top-right corner of your Mac screen (next to WiFi, battery, clock). OmniPilot lives there — always running quietly, one click away. This is perfect for an always-on assistant because:

  • It doesn't clutter your Dock
  • It's always accessible with one click
  • It runs in the background without a visible window
  • You can trigger it with a keyboard shortcut (Cmd+Shift+O)

Technology 2: Whisper (Ears)

Real-world analogy

Whisper is like a human transcriptionist sitting next to you. You speak, they write down every word. Except Whisper does it in 200 milliseconds (0.2 seconds) and works in 99 languages.

What Is Speech-to-Text (STT)?

Speech-to-Text is the technology that converts spoken words into written text. When you say "Schedule a meeting tomorrow," STT produces the text "Schedule a meeting tomorrow." This is what Siri, Google Assistant, and Alexa use to understand you.

Why we chose Whisper

Privacy is the #1 reason. OmniPilot listens to all your conversations. If we sent that audio to Google or Apple, every word you say would be on their servers. With Whisper running locally, your audio never leaves your Mac. Period.

What Is whisper.cpp?

Whisper was originally written in Python by OpenAI. whisper.cpp is a rewrite in C++ that runs 10x faster and uses far less memory. On your M1 Mac, it uses Apple's CoreML (the chip's built-in AI accelerator) to run even faster.

What Is a "Model" (ggml-small.en.bin)?

Key Concept: AI Models

An AI model is a file that contains everything the AI has learned. Think of it as a very compressed brain. The ggml-small.en.bin file (465MB) contains all the patterns Whisper learned from listening to 680,000 hours of audio. When you speak, Whisper compares your audio against these patterns to figure out what words you said.

Model sizes are a tradeoff:

  • Tiny (75MB) — Fast but makes more mistakes
  • Small (465MB) — Good balance of speed and accuracy (what we use)
  • Large (2.9GB) — Most accurate but slower

Technology 3: VAD (The Filter)

Real-world analogy

VAD is like a receptionist at the front desk. They don't write down everything — they only call the transcriptionist (Whisper) when someone actually starts talking. If the room is silent, the receptionist does nothing. This saves energy and processing power.

What Is Voice Activity Detection?

VAD = Voice Activity Detection. It answers one simple question: "Is someone speaking right now, or is it just silence/noise?"

Without VAD, Whisper would try to transcribe every second of audio — including silence, fan noise, music, and dog barks. That wastes CPU and produces garbage text like "[BLANK AUDIO]" or "(music playing)."

How Our VAD Works

Audio comes in (30ms chunks, 480 samples each) | v [Calculate energy level (RMS)] ← "How loud is this?" | v Is energy > threshold? | | Yes No | | "Speech!" "Silence" | | Buffer it Discard it | v When speech stops (15 silent frames): Send the buffered speech to Whisper
What is RMS?

RMS = Root Mean Square. It's a way to measure "how loud" audio is. Silence has an RMS near 0. Speaking has an RMS around 0.01-0.1. Shouting is 0.3+. Our threshold is 0.01 — anything above this is considered speech.

We use Apple's Accelerate framework to compute this, which uses the M1 chip's special math hardware to do it in microseconds.

Why Not Use Silero VAD?

Silero VAD is a neural network-based VAD (more accurate than energy-based). We'll upgrade to it later — it requires loading an ONNX model. Our simple energy-based VAD works well enough for v1 and uses less than 0.1% CPU.

Technology 4: Ollama + LLM (The Brain)

Real-world analogy

If Whisper is the ears (hearing what you say), the LLM is the brain (understanding what it means). The LLM reads your transcriptions and can summarize them, extract important details, and answer your questions about past conversations.

What Is an LLM?

Key Concept: Large Language Model

LLM = Large Language Model. It's the technology behind ChatGPT, Claude, and Google Gemini. An LLM is trained on billions of pages of text and learns to predict what words should come next. This simple ability (next-word prediction) gives it the power to summarize, translate, answer questions, write code, and more.

Think of it as a very well-read person who has read every book, article, and website — and can now have a conversation about anything.

What Is Ollama?

Ollama is a tool that lets you run LLMs on your own computer. Without Ollama, you'd need to use ChatGPT (which sends your data to OpenAI's servers). Ollama downloads the AI model to your Mac and runs it locally.

Why Llama 3.2 (3B)?

Model choice explained

We chose Meta's Llama 3.2 at 3 billion parameters. Here's what that means:

  • "3 billion parameters" = the model has 3 billion adjustable settings that were tuned during training. More parameters = smarter but slower and bigger
  • Size: 2GB on disk — fits comfortably on your Mac with room to spare
  • Speed: 20-30 words/second — fast enough for real-time conversation
  • We initially tried Qwen3 8B (8 billion parameters, 5.2GB) but it was too large for your disk and uses "thinking mode" which made it slow for simple tasks

For OmniPilot's tasks (summarize, extract names, answer questions), 3B is plenty smart. We're not writing novels — we're finding who said what in a meeting.

Technology 5: SQLite (The Memory)

Real-world analogy

SQLite is like a filing cabinet where all your memories are stored. Every conversation gets a folder (with date, who was talking, what topics were discussed), and you can search through all folders instantly. Except this filing cabinet is a single file on your Mac — about the size of a photo.

What Is a Database?

A database is a structured way to store and find information. Instead of dumping everything in one big text file, a database organizes data into tables (like Excel spreadsheets) so you can search, filter, and sort instantly.

What Is FTS5 (Full-Text Search)?

Key Concept

FTS5 = Full-Text Search, version 5. It's a SQLite feature that lets you search text like Google searches the web. When you ask "What did I discuss about budget?", FTS5 finds every memory containing the word "budget" in milliseconds — even if you have thousands of memories.

Without FTS5, searching would mean reading every single memory one by one (slow). With FTS5, it builds an index (like a book's index at the back) so lookups are instant.

Our Memory Schema (How Data Is Organized)

Each "memory" stores: what was said (content), when (timestamp), who (participants), about what (topics), and how important (score). This lets you search by any dimension.

Key AI Concepts You Now Own

1. On-Device AI vs Cloud AI

The most important concept

Most AI products (ChatGPT, Siri, Alexa) run in the cloud — your data is sent to someone else's computer, processed there, and results sent back. OmniPilot runs entirely on your Mac. No internet needed. No data shared. This is called "on-device AI" or "local AI" or "edge AI."

Why this matters for a personal assistant: OmniPilot hears everything you say. If that data went to a cloud, someone could potentially access all your conversations. Local = private by design.

2. Tokens & Parameters

LLM vocabulary

Token: A piece of a word. "OmniPilot" might be 3 tokens: "Omni" + "Pil" + "ot". LLMs think in tokens, not words. When we say "20-30 tokens/second," that's roughly 15-22 words/second.

Parameters: The "knowledge settings" in an AI model. Llama 3.2 has 3 billion of these. More parameters = more knowledge and reasoning ability, but also more RAM and slower speed.

3. Embeddings & Vector Search

Future upgrade (coming in Sprint 2)

Embedding: Converting text into a list of numbers (a "vector") that captures its meaning, not just the words. "Happy" and "joyful" would have very similar number lists, even though they're different words.

Vector search: Finding similar meanings, not just matching keywords. When you search "budget discussion," vector search also finds memories about "financial planning" and "cost estimates" even if they don't contain the word "budget."

Currently we use keyword search (FTS5). Vector search will make OmniPilot much smarter at finding relevant memories.

4. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

What OmniPilot actually does when you ask a question

RAG is a technique where instead of asking the LLM to answer from its training data (which knows nothing about YOUR conversations), we first retrieve relevant memories from the database, then give them to the LLM as context, and ask it to answer based on those.

Without RAG: "What did I discuss about budget?" → LLM guesses (wrong).
With RAG: "Here are 5 memories mentioning budget. Now answer the question." → LLM gives an accurate, sourced answer.
