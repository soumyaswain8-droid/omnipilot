The Hinglish gap

The AI note-taker category has one clear winner, several corpses, and a hole in it shaped exactly like the bug we measured on our own audio three weeks ago.

27 Aug 2026 Surya AI / Sidewall 6 research agents Internal

01 — The finding

The gap is not Hindi. It is code-switching.

This matters because the two lead to completely different products. Hindi transcription is already well served — vendors claim above 95% accuracy, one reports 5.7% word error rate. If the pitch were "we do Hindi," we would be entering a solved market late.

What breaks is Hindi and English mixed inside a single sentence, which is how Indian professionals actually talk.

Each region has its own code-switching pattern, and each one breaks global AI transcription tools that expect a single language setting.

— transcription-tool market review, 2026

We have first-hand evidence, not a vendor claim. On 30 seconds of a real Hindi-English webinar recorded on 9 August, the English-only model returned 33 characters — seven words. The multilingual model returned 481. Same audio, same machine, same minute.

And the timing is unusually good

Indic-Transcribe launches on 5 September 2026 — nine days from now. Built by IIT Madras / AI4Bharat with BodhanAI, 1.2 billion parameters, 26 Indian languages. Critically it ships a "Flex" variant built specifically for code-mixed and Romanized text. That is a purpose-built model for the exact failure we measured, arriving in a market where no AI wearable ships any Indic support at all.

03 — Capture reality

Where you can actually record, and where you can't

Each platform has a hard ceiling, and in most cases it is set by store policy rather than engineering. This is the constraint that decides what gets built.

Mobile cannot tap call audio. At all. Android closed it at version 10 and Google banned the Accessibility-API workaround in 2022; CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT is signature-privileged and unavailable to Play apps. iOS has no API. Every product handles this identically — Otter, Plaud mobile and the Limitless pendant all just record ambient audio and ask you to use speakerphone.

Web is stronger than expected but Chromium-only. Chrome and Edge can capture tab audio, so a web client gets both sides of a Meet or Zoom-web call with no bot and no download. Firefox and Safari return no audio at all. Capturing a desktop Zoom app from a browser on macOS is impossible without a virtual audio driver.

Which reframes what a mobile app would be

Not "OmniPilot on your phone" for online meetings — that path is closed by policy. A mobile app is a recorder for in-person conversation: client visits, site inspections, doctor consultations, lectures, field sales. That is a different market from online meetings, and notably it is the one where Indian code-switching is most intense and where our screen-OCR advantage disappears entirely.

04 — Incumbent weakness

What people actually complain about

Worth noting because it is not what you would guess. Accuracy is not the top complaint — billing is.

  • Subscription dark patterns dominate the one-star reviews. Repeated, independent: "the button to cancel subscription did not work", followed by "we do not provide refunds on monthly subscriptions." This is a trust wound across the category, and it is free to avoid.
  • Crosstalk is the real accuracy failure. Clean-audio accuracy of 95–98% falls below 80% on real-world audio. Users describe brainstorms attributed to a hallucinated "Speaker 5" the moment two people talk at once.
  • Free-tier erosion drives churn. "I have been an Otter user since 2021 and they keep tightening the free tier while the paid price keeps going up."
  • Granola's success is instructive — it is liked specifically because it doesn't join calls as a visible bot. Same architectural choice we made.

The hallucination problem is quantified, and we already built the fix

Research on Whisper found roughly 1% of transcriptions contain entirely fabricated content nobody said — and of those fabrications, 38% carried explicit harms: invented violence, false attributions, fabricated authority. That is the industry-wide version of what happened to us on 15 July, when two hours of near-silence produced a confident, wholly fictional set of notes.

We now refuse to summarise below a speech threshold, and we mark every figure [OCR] or [audio] so the reader knows what to trust. No competitor does either. Against a documented, quantified, harm-carrying industry failure, that is a real claim — not a feature bullet.

05 — The cliff

Always-on wearables are the worst legal position available

A meeting has known participants who can be asked. A pendant in a shop, a bar or someone's living room captures strangers with no notice mechanism at all. That is a categorically harder problem than the one we already flagged for the desktop recorder.

  • Twelve US states require all-party consent. One bystander in any of them and every recording is exposed.
  • California SB 1130, amended 13 August 2026, is the first law written specifically for wearables — fines to $10,000, and it criminalizes disabling the recording indicator light. It legislates the LED itself.
  • GDPR's household exemption collapses the moment the device leaves the home (Ryneš), so there is no "personal use" shelter for a wearable.
  • The social backlash is physical. Friend's subway campaign had 90+ ads defaced — "SURVEILLANCE CAPITALISM." Soho House, Wetherspoons and ATG Theatres have all restricted smart glasses, treating them as hidden cameras. The Google Glass script is replaying a decade later.

India is the exception, and it cuts both ways. Under DPDP as currently drafted, a wearer need not disclose recording to bystanders, and bystanders have no account and therefore no access, correction or erasure rights. That is permissive today. It is also a gap that could close quickly, and building a business on a rule likely to tighten is a poor bet.

One legitimate carve-out exists

SB 1130 explicitly exempts disability-related devices. The deaf and hard-of-hearing market is real and funded — Rylo has raised over $100M, Ava $10M. But be honest about what it is: live captioning for face-to-face conversation is a different product from post-hoc meeting summarisation, with a different buyer. It is an adjacent business, not a positioning trick to dodge consent law.

06 — Options

Three ways in

Option A Build a wearable

Fork Omi's open hardware ($89 dev kit, 28 repos) rather than starting from scratch. Pair it with Indic transcription.

Hardware supply chain, certification, returns, support — none of which we have done. Worst legal exposure. Competing with Plaud's two-million-unit head start and Meta and Amazon buying the rest.

Not now. Revisit if software proves demand.
Option B — recommended Hinglish-native software

Mobile app for in-person conversation, web client for browser meetings, existing Mac app for desktop calls. One backend, code-switching-native, sourced figures, no dark patterns.

Uses what exists: 77% of the Swift Meetings module ports to iOS unchanged. No hardware risk. Legal exposure is the manageable meeting kind, not the bystander kind.

Start here.
Option C Vertical scribe

Pick one high-value profession — small-clinic doctors, lawyers, field sales — and build the whole workflow, not just notes.

Highest revenue per user in the entire category; Abridge is valued at $5.3B on clinical scribing. But it needs domain depth, and clinical carries its own regulatory weight.

The real prize. Needs a design partner first.

Option B and Option C are sequential, not alternatives. Build the Hinglish-native capture layer, find which profession pulls hardest on it, then go deep on that one.

07 — Next two weeks

What would actually de-risk this

  • 5 September: test Indic-Transcribe the day it launches. Run it against the 9 August capture already on disk. The entire thesis rests on code-switching being solvable — this is the cheapest possible test of it, and the benchmark audio exists.
  • Record five in-person conversations on a phone. Ambient mic, no special tooling. If mobile capture quality is unusable for real Indian meeting rooms, Option B dies early and cheaply.
  • Talk to five people who'd pay. Consultants, clinic doctors, field sales. Not "would you use this" — "what do you do today, and what does it cost you." Nobody outside this building has asked for any of this yet.
  • Price against the known floor. The category's centre of gravity is $15–25/month; Plaud Pro is $17.99. An India-first price of ₹499–999 sits well under it with room for real margin.

The one-line version

Don't build a wearable. Build the thing that understands how Indians actually speak — on a phone, in a browser, and on the Mac tool that already works — and let the first profession that pulls hardest tell you what to build next.
