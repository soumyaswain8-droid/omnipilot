01 — Bottom line up front

We cannot out-feature Read AI. We can out-position it.

Read AI is a funded, four-tier product spanning meetings, Gmail, chat and cross-platform search. Matching its surface area is not a realistic goal for a side-built Mac app. But three things have moved in the last six months that open a genuine, narrow lane.

The lane

Bots are being locked out. Microsoft Teams began blocking and labelling third-party recording bots in 2026 (targeted rollout mid-May, GA early June). Google Meet now flags third-party notetakers as a "potential risk" and denies entry by default. Every cloud notetaker in the category — Read AI, Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, tl;dv — depends on a bot getting into the room. Device-level capture doesn't ask permission from the platform.

The moat

Our marginal cost per meeting hour is zero. Read AI charges $15–$39.75 per seat per month. A bot-based competitor building today pays Recall.ai $0.50 per recording-hour before it earns a rupee. Whisper and Ollama on an M-series Mac cost electricity. That is a structural pricing advantage nobody in the category can copy without abandoning their architecture.

The unfair advantage we already built by accident

Screen OCR. Across our three real captures, OCR carried more weight than the audio: every reliable financial figure in the Aug 9 webinar note, every link in the Aug 6 call after the audio died at 17:03, and the only trustworthy content from the failed July capture. Granola — the one true local-first competitor — is audio-only, transcript-only, no video, no OCR. Read AI's screen intelligence is behind a bot.

Against that, one correction to an assumption worth killing early:

Local-first is not a legal shield

Granola is currently defending a US federal class action alleging wiretapping — on facts commentators call worse than Otter's, precisely because there is no visible bot and therefore no notice to other participants. Visibility isn't a shield either (Otter is also being sued; motion-to-dismiss hearing 20 May 2026). Local capture is a capability and cost advantage. Sold as a privacy advantage without an explicit disclosure mechanism, it is a liability.

Recommendation: build it, but scope it as an India-first prosumer and SMB tool with consent designed in from day one — not an enterprise Read AI replacement. The reasoning runs through the rest of this document.

Anatomy of their Meeting Report

This is the artefact we are actually competing with — the thing a user opens after the call. Eight components:

  • Summary — key points, decisions, takeaways
  • Key discussion points — clickable chapters that jump to the moment
  • Action items — auto-detected, plus key questions and their answers
  • Annotated transcript — full dialogue overlaid with participant reactions
  • Audience reactions — pivotal moments flagged by engagement
  • Playback & highlight reel — video replay, auto-cut highlights Enterprise only
  • Participant deep dive — talk time, "bias" and "charisma" per speaker
  • Coaching metrics — live talking-speed and filler-word tracking

Where their moat is thinner than it looks

The talk-time / bias / charisma / coaching layer all depends on knowing who spoke. Bots get that for free — Zoom RTMS, the Meet Media API and Teams Graph hand back per-participant audio streams already labelled by speaker. It is not a smarter diarization model; it is a structural gift from the platform API. Any local single-microphone product must earn the same labels with ML diarization at roughly 12–14% error. This is the one gap we cannot close by working harder. Design around it rather than pretending otherwise.

Two claims of theirs did not survive verification and should not be cited back at us in a sales conversation: Smart Scheduler's actual mechanics (referenced only in passing, no feature page found) and the existence of a persistent team-level analytics dashboard (marketed in prose, no UI evidence found). Webex support is claimed on one page and absent from their integrations page.

06 — Gap analysis

Five gaps that actually matter

Ordered by leverage, not by difficulty. Everything else on the matrix is downstream of these.

Gap 1 — We only hear one side of the room

The single biggest functional hole, and the cause of the July total failure. The fix is designed and waiting: a Core Audio process tap (driver-free, macOS 14.4+, no BlackHole, no admin install) capturing the system output mix, live-mixed with the default mic into one WAV — which meeting-notes.sh already knows how to watch. Zero downstream changes. The design has been paused awaiting approval for 35 days.

Gap 2 — The transcript is running the wrong model

ggml-small.en cannot process Hindi. Move to large-v3-turbo. Longer term, WhisperKit (Argmax, CoreML/ANE-native) is the strongest macOS path — 1.3–1.8× faster than raw Metal, and since v1.0 it bundles SpeakerKit, which matches pyannote's error rate across 13 datasets at roughly an order of magnitude less compute. That single SDK closes transcription speed and diarization together.

Gap 3 — Nobody knows who said what

Without speaker labels we cannot produce action items attributed to a person, talk-time analytics, or a sales-call summary worth paying for. Options that run offline on an M1: pyannote.audio 3.1 (MIT-licensed, free for commercial use, pure PyTorch, CPU-only viable, ~12–14% DER on AMI) or SpeakerKit if we adopt WhisperKit. We will not match bot-grade attribution. We can get close enough for a two-to-six person call, which is the meeting shape our buyer actually has.

Gap 4 — Notes are prose, not a report

Our notes are genuinely good — arguably better written than Read AI's template output, because they carry source-confidence markers ([OCR] vs [audio]) that Read AI has no equivalent for. But they are a wall of markdown. Read AI's report is navigable: chapters that jump to a timestamp, action items as objects, reactions overlaid on the transcript. We have the timestamps already. This is a rendering problem, not a capture problem.

Keep the confidence markers — they are a real feature

Our Aug 9 note distinguishes numbers read off-screen via OCR (reliable) from numbers heard via an English-only model (not). Read AI has no such concept; a hallucinated figure arrives with the same confidence as a real one. After the July confabulation incident we know exactly why that matters. "Every number is sourced" is a sellable claim no competitor makes.

Gap 5 — The summarising model is too small

llama3.2:3b is the weakest link in the note quality chain, and it wrote the July hallucination. qwen2.5:7b beats Llama-3.1-8B on multilingual work and matches it on English reasoning — the right pick for Hindi-English meeting content. On this 16 GB M1, run it via MLX rather than Ollama; Ollama's MLX backend needs 32 GB before it delivers its speedup.

07 — Non-negotiable

The consent problem is a product requirement

This determines whether the thing is sellable at all, so it belongs in the design rather than the terms of service.

In US all-party-consent states — California, Illinois, Florida, Pennsylvania, Washington, Massachusetts, Maryland and roughly four to six more depending on source — jurisdiction attaches to where the recorded person sits, not where the recorder sits. There is no "the organiser agreed" defence covering the other participants. A silent recorder with no indicator is exposed by default the moment any participant is in one of those states.

India is materially softer: a participant recording their own call is generally legal, and the IT Act imposes no all-party requirement. The real Indian obligation is DPDP Act 2023, which attaches once we store and process other people's voices and statements — requiring itemised notice, stated purpose and retention, and a consent-withdrawal mechanism. Phase 3 compliance lands 13 May 2027, so there is runway, but the product must be built to satisfy it rather than retrofitted.

Turn the obligation into the differentiator

Nobody in this category has solved disclosure well — that is exactly why Otter and Granola are both in court. A local recorder that makes announcing itself effortless (a one-tap "recording notice" posted into the meeting chat, a visible menu-bar state, a spoken or pasted disclosure, per-participant retention controls) is doing something genuinely novel, and it is a claim we can defend in a sales conversation against every competitor named in this document.

Minimum for v1: visible recording state in the menu bar, one-click disclosure text to paste into chat, a default retention window with automatic deletion, and an explicit "this call includes participants outside India" warning path. None of this blocks the engineering work — it runs alongside.

This is regulatory research, not legal advice. The state list, the DPDP phase status and the GDPR lawful-basis choice all need a licensed attorney before we sell to anyone outside India.

08 — Execution

Build plan

Four phases. The first two are largely assembly of parts that already exist; the value only becomes sellable at M3.

M0 Stop the bleeding ~1 day
  • Add data/ to .gitignore before anything else touches the repo
  • Commit the five working shell scripts — they are the product
  • Decide: merge feat/meetings-m1 or park it explicitly
  • Swap ggml-small.en → large-v3-turbo
  • Swap llama3.2:3b → qwen2.5:7b via MLX
  • Fix the whisper-server pkill pattern in stop

Outcome: nothing is at risk, Hindi works, notes get better. No new architecture.

M1 Hear the whole room ~1–2 weeks
  • Build omni-audiotap — Core Audio process tap, ~150 lines Swift
  • Mix tap + mic into one WAV via ffmpeg amix
  • meeting-ctl.sh start with pre-flight: disk, devices, whisper health
  • Thin-transcript guard — refuse to summarise below a speech threshold
  • Raise deployment target to macOS 14.4
  • Loud degradation warning if the tap is unavailable

Outcome: the July failure becomes structurally impossible. Parity with Granola on capture.

M2 Know who spoke, and record the screen ~2–3 weeks
  • Adopt WhisperKit + SpeakerKit, or pyannote 3.1 as the offline fallback
  • Speaker-attributed transcript feeding the summariser
  • Promote video capture from shell to ScreenCaptureKit
  • Keep the OCR loop — it is the differentiator
  • Structured action items as objects, not prose

Outcome: reports become attributable. Talk-time analytics unlock.

M3 The report people pay for ~2–3 weeks
  • Navigable report UI: chapters jumping to timestamps
  • Source-confidence badges on every figure — OCR vs audio
  • Consent layer: menu-bar state, one-click disclosure, retention policy
  • Export: PDF, markdown, shareable link
  • Push action items to Todoist or Asana (single-token auth)

Outcome: first sellable version. This is the demo.

M4 Calendar, reminders, scheduling ~1–2 weeks
  • EventKit read — no OAuth, no CASA, no Google review
  • Parse events for meeting URLs: conferenceData first, regex on location/notes as fallback
  • UNCalendarNotificationTrigger at start−5min with a "Start recording" action button
  • Arm capture automatically when a detected meeting begins
  • Write-back via EventKit save() — syncs to Google/Exchange through macOS itself

Outcome: reminders + scheduling, using the TaskScheduler we already ship.

M5 Optional — the bot path ~1 week
  • Recall.ai at $0.50/recording-hour, no platform fee since 2026
  • Skip their transcription add-on — pull raw audio to local whisper
  • Price as pass-through, not absorbed
  • Only for meetings the user cannot attend in person

Outcome: covers "record it without me" without breaking the cost model.

The EventKit shortcut is worth calling out separately

Because macOS syncs Google and Exchange accounts into Calendar.app at OS level, an EventKit app reads Google Meet and Teams events with zero cloud OAuth. That skips Google's restricted-scope review and the annual CASA assessment entirely — which runs $500–$4,500 for the self-assessment path and into five figures for a full third-party audit, plus weeks of launch delay. For a local Mac app this is a genuine structural advantage over any web-based competitor. The one caveat: it only works if the user has added their account to Calendar.app rather than living in a browser tab.

09 — Commercial

Can we actually sell this?

Applying the standard test before any of the above gets built: who is the customer, will they pay, how do they find us, what is the smallest thing we can charge for, and what is the weekly target?

Who pays

Not enterprises. SOC 2 Type II is a 9–14 month, $25–80k prerequisite, and Type II specifically requires several months of live control operation before the audit can even begin. That gate is real and it is not worth walking through for a v1.

The buyer is Indian consultants, agencies, solo founders and small sales teams who run Hindi-English calls. This segment is badly served today: Read AI's free tier caps at five meetings a month and one hour, its paid tier starts at $15/seat (roughly ₹1,300), and the whole category transcribes code-switched Hindi-English poorly. They also do exactly the kind of call — screen-shared decks, numbers on screen, pasted links — where our OCR layer beats everyone.

Distribution

The honest answer is that we do not have one yet, and that is the actual risk — not the engineering. The cheapest credible test: a landing page with the ₹599 price and a waitlist, before M2 is finished. If nobody joins the waitlist, the remaining phases should not be built. Beyond that, the natural channels are the existing LaunchGate portfolio, Indian founder and consultant communities, and a public build narrative around "the meeting recorder that works when Teams blocks the bots."

Test the market before M3, not after

M0 through M2 is roughly four to six weeks of work that improves a tool we already use daily — that is defensible regardless of commercial outcome. M3 onward is product investment that only pays off if the buyer exists. Put the landing page up during M1 and let signups decide whether M3 happens.

10 — Pre-mortem

What would kill this

  • Apple ships it. macOS 26 already added SpeechAnalyzer / SpeechTranscriber, purpose-built for long-form audio, with Apple claiming 55% faster than Whisper. A native system-level meeting recorder in macOS 27 would end this category for third parties. Mitigation: that same API is also ours to adopt — it would make us faster, and the OCR, reporting and consent layers stay differentiated.
  • The litigation goes badly. If the Granola or Otter cases establish that silent local capture is wiretapping, the whole local-first category takes damage. Mitigation: the consent layer in M3, and India-first positioning where the participant-recording question is settled.
  • Speaker attribution stays too weak to sell. ~12–14% DER may not be good enough for a sales-call product where "who committed to what" is the value. Mitigation: test on real calls at M2 before building M3 on top of it.
  • Nobody wants to install a Mac app. The whole category is browser-and-cloud for a reason. A native app is a harder install and a Mac-only market. Mitigation: this is precisely what the waitlist test measures.
  • It stays a side project. The most likely failure. This document describes six to ten weeks of focused work; against 60-plus other projects it becomes another unmerged branch.
11 — Over to you

Five decisions only you can make

  1. 01 Do M0 now?

    Two commands close both standing risks — data/ ignored, working scripts committed. The model swaps take an afternoon and fix Hindi. There is no argument against this one.

  2. 02 Approve the paused both-sides capture design?

    It has waited 35 days. It needs one correction — the macOS floor is 14.4, not 14.2 — and then it can go to spec. Until it lands, every capture repeats the July failure mode.

  3. 03 Merge feat/meetings-m1, or make shell the product?

    Right now we maintain both and benefit from neither. My read: keep shell as the working pipeline through M1, and port to Swift at M2 when WhisperKit forces the issue anyway. But it should be a stated decision, not drift.

  4. 04 Is this a product or an internal tool?

    Internal tool: stop after M1, and it is worth it. Product: M0–M4 is six to ten weeks and needs the landing-page test running by M1. Both are legitimate. Choosing neither is what produces another unmerged branch.

  5. 05 India-first, or chase the global market?

    India-first means the consent question is manageable, the Hindi gap becomes our moat rather than our bug, and ₹599 is a real price. Global means US all-party-consent exposure and a SOC 2 clock before anyone enterprise will talk to us.
